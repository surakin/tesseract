Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-09-19

Tesseract is a Matrix chat client. It is not a hosted service: there is no Tesseract server, and the people who develop Tesseract do not operate any infrastructure that your account data, messages, or media pass through. This page describes what Tesseract stores on your device and what it sends over the network, and to whom.

Data stored on your device

Tesseract stores your login session, encryption keys, and a local cache of rooms and messages on your device, alongside your app settings. None of this is collected by or transmitted to the Tesseract developers. On Windows this lives under %APPDATA%\Tesseract; on macOS under ~/Library/Application Support/Tesseract; on Linux under ~/.local/share/tesseract (settings under ~/.config/tesseract).

Tesseract also keeps a separate, expendable cache of media you've viewed — downloaded images, stickers, video thumbnails, map tiles, and voice-message waveforms — so it doesn't have to re-download them each time. This cache can be cleared at any time from Settings, and is stored under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tesseract on Windows, ~/Library/Caches/Tesseract on macOS, and ~/.cache/tesseract on Linux.

Data sent over the network

Tesseract communicates directly with the Matrix homeserver you log in with — the one you or your account's administrator chose, not one operated by the Tesseract developers. That connection carries your login/authentication, your messages, room state, and any media you send or receive, exactly as it would for any other Matrix client connecting to that homeserver. If you communicate with users on other homeservers, your messages are federated to those homeservers by your own homeserver, per the Matrix protocol.

Login via Matrix Authentication Service (MAS) uses a loopback redirect handled entirely on your own device; the authentication exchange itself happens directly between your device and your homeserver's own OAuth server.

Microphone and camera

Tesseract only accesses your microphone or camera when you start or join a voice/video call. That media is sent to the call infrastructure (SFU) provided by the homeserver(s) involved in the call — never to the Tesseract developers.

Crash reports

Tesseract can optionally write a local crash report file to help you diagnose a problem. This is off by default, and even when enabled, Tesseract does not automatically upload or transmit these reports anywhere — they stay on your device unless you choose to share one yourself, for example by attaching it to a GitHub issue.

Third parties

Your homeserver operator, and any call/SFU infrastructure it uses, are third parties independent of Tesseract's developers. Their own privacy practices govern the data your account sends to them. Check with your homeserver administrator if you have questions about how your homeserver handles your data.

Children's privacy

Tesseract is not directed at children under 13, and the Tesseract developers do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, regardless of age — see above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the update will be posted on this page.

Contact

Questions about this policy or about Tesseract can be raised via the project's GitHub repository: github.com/surakin/tesseract.
